# Business problem: what does concentrated catalogue viewing mean?

## The concern

Total viewing can look healthy while attention sits on a small part of the catalogue. Total viewing alone cannot tell a business whether the rest of that catalogue is useful choice, catalogue supply that was not realistically reachable, content people tried and dropped, or genuine room for more viewing.

That is the concern motivating this case — not a diagnosis already made.

## The question that replaced "which titles are under-watched?"

> What explains concentrated catalogue consumption among active viewers, and which patterns represent genuine unrealised viewing opportunity rather than healthy preference-driven behaviour?

The first instinct is to rank titles by how little they are watched. But a title's low total identifies neither a failure, nor an audience that would watch it, nor an intervention that would change anything. Ranking skips the middle of the argument.

So the analysis follows **concentration → mechanism → opportunity**: establish how attention is allocated, then work out which behavioural and access conditions explain it, and only then ask whether more viewing is realistically achievable.

## Why the tempting shortcuts fail

Each shortcut below produces a number that looks like utilisation and quietly measures something else.

| Tempting shortcut | What it actually confuses | Distinction required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles watched ÷ whole catalogue | The platform's inventory with a viewer's reachable choice | Historical entitlement, availability and profile tenure |
| Number of assets played | Episodes within one programme with exploration across programmes | Episodic depth versus parent-title breadth |
| Low breadth = disengagement | Healthy loyalty with weak engagement | Depth, repeat activity and meaningful engagement alongside breadth |
| Account viewing = one viewer | Account-level viewing context with one behavioural identity | Profile-level behaviour with account-level context |
| Current plan explains past viewing | A later commercial state with the access held at the time | Effective access on the date being measured |
| Every unfinished start is abandonment | Follow-up not yet observed with an outcome actually seen | Legitimate denominators and explicit unknowns |

Movies and episodic programmes also produce different shapes of engagement. Ten episodes of one series can be sustained preference; ten different titles are a different kind of breadth. Neither is better until it is tied back to the business question.

Language needs the same care. A title's original language, the audio languages it offers and the language someone actually listened to are three separate facts. Supply never proves consumption.

## What this analysis can and cannot decide

The aim is to focus effort where more catalogue viewing is plausibly achievable. Healthy, preference-driven concentration may need no intervention at all. Weak engagement, sampling that never settles, and restricted access each point to a different investigation.

Any headroom identified will be a **diagnosis, not a causal estimate** of what an action would achieve. There are no recommendation impression, ranking, search or carousel logs, so the analysis cannot see what viewers were shown or attribute viewing to where a title was placed.

## Where this stands

The framing, data model and mart architecture are in place, and the viewer-level measurement base has been audited through grain, activity, breadth, concentration and post-choice response. Candidate behavioural mechanisms have been established beneath similar concentration and read against the catalogue each profile could realistically reach, and cross-origin catalogue propensity has been tested for redundancy against those mechanisms and provisionally retained as a cross-cutting dimension.

What that is not: no segment definitions, intervention priorities, headroom estimates or business findings exist yet. The evidence so far describes behaviour within two 90-day observation windows. Whether the latest observed mechanisms persist, how profiles arrived at them, and where concentration leaves credible room for additional meaningful viewing are the open questions, and the next step is within-profile longitudinal analysis rather than any business recommendation.
